---
title: Generating a compliance seal
description: What the seal proves, how to share it with an auditor, and verification steps.
---

A **compliance seal** is a cryptographically signed attestation that all agent decisions in a specified period were governed by a published policy. It's a compact, verifiable proof you can give to auditors without sharing raw evidence data.

## What the seal proves

A seal certifies that:
- All decisions in the specified time window had policy coverage (compliance rate = 100%)
- The policy revisions in effect during the period were reviewed and approved
- The evidence records have not been tampered with (the seal includes a Merkle root of the evidence dataset)

A seal cannot be generated if the compliance rate for the period is below 100% — provenance gaps must be resolved first.

## Generating a seal

<Steps>
<Step>

Apply date and filter settings to match the period you want to attest. The compliance rate must show 100% — if it doesn't, resolve gaps before proceeding.

</Step>
<Step>

Click **Generate seal** in the report header.

</Step>
<Step>

The seal dialog shows:
- **Period** — start and end timestamps
- **Decision count** — number of decisions covered
- **Policy revisions** — the revision IDs in effect during the period
- **Compliance rate** — must be 100%

Review these carefully before signing.

</Step>
<Step>

Click **Sign and generate**. Spctre generates the seal. This may take a few seconds for large periods.

</Step>
<Step>

The seal is created with a unique **Seal ID** (e.g. `seal_01j9xk…`) and a **seal package** containing:
- A JWT-signed seal document
- The Merkle root of evidence for the period
- The public key used for signing

</Step>
</Steps>

## Sharing with an auditor

Download the seal package as a JSON file and provide it to your auditor. Include:
- The seal package JSON
- Instructions to verify using the Spctre public key (shown on the seal detail page)

## Verifying a seal

Auditors can verify a seal without accessing your Spctre instance:

1. Decode the JWT using the Spctre public key (available at your control plane's `/.well-known/spctre-seal.json`)
2. Verify the Merkle root against the exported evidence dataset
3. Confirm the period, decision count, and compliance rate in the decoded payload

Spctre also provides a self-contained **seal verifier CLI** for auditors:

```bash
npx @spctre/verify-seal --seal seal_package.json --pubkey spctre-seal.json
```

## Seal storage

All generated seals are stored in **Compliance → Seals**. Seals cannot be deleted or modified after creation. They are included in compliance exports when the relevant period is exported.
