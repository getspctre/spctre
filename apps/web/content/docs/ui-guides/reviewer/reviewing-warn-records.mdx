---
title: Reviewing a WARN record
description: What WARN means, adding a note, and marking as acknowledged.
---

A `WARN` decision means the agent's action was permitted by policy, but a rule flagged it as worth reviewing. The action already happened — you're doing a post-hoc review, not approving or blocking.

## Opening a WARN record

From **Decision evidence**, click any row with a `WARN` decision to open the full record.

## What the record contains

### Decision summary

- **Agent** — name and ID of the agent instance
- **Connector** — the external service called
- **Action** — the specific operation (e.g. `charge`, `send_message`, `create_issue`)
- **Timestamp** — when the decision was made
- **Decision ID** — immutable identifier, useful for support escalation

### Matched rule

The rule that triggered the `WARN`:
- Rule title and stable rule ID
- The conditions that matched
- The policy branch and revision that contained the rule

### Decision payload

The full JSON evidence payload the agent submitted. Expand individual fields to read values. Use this to assess whether the flagged action was appropriate in context.

### Agent context

If the agent included additional context fields (e.g. `conversation_id`, `user_id`, `session_start`), they appear here.

## Assessing the record

Ask yourself:
1. Given the payload, was this action appropriate?
2. Is this a one-off, or do I see this pattern repeatedly?
3. Should the policy rule be tightened to prevent or escalate this instead of warning?

## Adding a note

Click **Add note** to record your assessment. Notes appear in the audit trail and compliance reports. Good notes explain why you made the call you made — not just "looks fine."

## Acknowledging the record

Click **Acknowledge** to mark the record as reviewed. The status changes from `PENDING` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`. This is the normal close action — it means "I've seen this and it's acceptable."

Acknowledged records are still visible in the queue (filter by status) and are included in compliance exports.

## Escalating manually

If the action looks like a policy failure or a risk you want a second opinion on, click **Escalate**. This creates a formal escalation record in **Agent governance → Escalations** and notifies the escalation reviewers for the workspace.

## Seeing patterns

If you're acknowledging many similar WARN records, that's a signal that the triggering rule may need revision. Click **View rule** from within the record to go directly to the rule in the policy branch — then work with a Policy Creator to tighten or escalate the condition.
